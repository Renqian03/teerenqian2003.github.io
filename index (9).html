I am a Ph.D. student in chemistry working on the photoelectrocatalytic (PEC) degradation of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in water. I received my B.S. in Agricultural Chemistry, with a minor in Chemistry, from National Taiwan University (GPA 3.89/4.3), where I developed visible-light photocatalysts in the Environmental Nanotechnology Lab under Prof. Shih Yang-Hsin. My work combines materials synthesis, spectroscopic and electrochemical characterisation, LC-MS analysis of degradation pathways and DFT calculations, and has contributed to four peer-reviewed journal articles.

Research

Current research: photoelectrocatalytic degradation of PFAS

Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances resist almost every conventional treatment because of their carbon–fluorine bonds. In my doctoral work I’m studying photoelectrocatalytic (PEC) systems that combine light and an applied bias to break these molecules down in water.

As an undergraduate in the Environmental Nanotechnology Lab at National Taiwan University (supervised by Prof. Shih Yang-Hsin), I designed and characterised nanomaterials that use visible light to degrade pollutants in water, pairing experiments with DFT calculations to understand charge transfer, reaction pathways and the toxicity of degradation products.

Lead-free perovskite–MOF photocatalysts

Hybrid catalysts for degrading antibiotics in water, supported by an NSTC Undergraduate Student Research Award.

Modified graphitic carbon nitride

Chlorine-doped, porous and template-assisted g-C₃N₄ for visible-light removal of bisphenol A, textile dyes and emerging organic pollutants.

Computational insight

DFT modelling with Gaussian, combined with LC-MS, to explain charge separation, map degradation pathways and assess intermediate toxicity.
